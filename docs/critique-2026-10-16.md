# MeChess against Lichess and Chess.com: a product critique

Date: 2026-10-16. Scope: review/analysis, learning, openings, insights and profile, play, navigation and layout.
Ideas and functionality only: nothing here suggests copying artwork, code, names or trademarks of either site.

## How this was done, and what to trust

* MeChess side: the docs (`docs/app.md`, `docs/ui.md`, `docs/game-analysis.md`), the pages under `frontend/src/features/*`, `frontend/src/components/*`, `frontend/src/board/*`, and the desktop and phone screenshots of the current build. The app was not run. Screenshots named below are the "current look" desktop set (review, coach, play, library, settings, palette, home) and the phone set (some phone shots still show the older monospace look and should be re-taken).
* Other sites: public pages and help articles only (Lichess features page, FAQ, openings page, "Learn from your mistakes" blog, Insights blog; Chess.com help center articles on Game Review, move classification, Insights, Practice, Bots; Chess.com features overview and the Game Review design announcement), plus forum threads and third-party comparison articles. Where a claim comes from a third-party mirror or a blog with a commercial interest, the finding says so. Where a detail could not be confirmed in public pages, it is marked "not verified" and is a suggestion, not a fact about the other site.
* Every finding gives: what the other site does (source), what MeChess does now (file or screenshot), why it matters, the change (sized for about a day or less unless marked L), priority (H/M/L) and effort (S/M/L).

## 1. Why it still feels "made by a beginner": the pattern behind the findings

Looking at all screens together, four things cause most of the impression, and they are cheaper to fix than any single feature:

1. **The board, the thing every chess player looks at first, is the weakest visual.** The default board theme is "Icy" with `grain: true` (`frontend/src/board/themes.ts`). In every screenshot the squares look like blurry clouds rather than crisp squares, and the coordinates sit on a noisy background. Both reference sites default to flat, high-contrast squares.
2. **Screens show numbers and empty states, not chess positions.** Library rows, Insights, Coach and Home are text and thin bars on white cards. Lichess's practice and puzzle lists and Chess.com's Insights ("click the board icon to list the positions", Chess.com help, "What is Insights") keep tying statistics to boards. MeChess has the data (every mistake has a position) but rarely draws it.
3. **The UI talks like a developer.** Examples in the code: "see docs/opening-explorer.md" and "Run `chessme books-learn` (docs/language-model.md)" in empty states, "Download the 3 to 5 piece files ... into a folder named syzygy", "Index the comments", "Your positions by box", "Add from my games". Neither site ever tells a player to use a terminal or a docs file. A first-time user meets these in the first minute because the Coach, Explorer and Endgames tabs are empty until data is built.
4. **Small type, thin cards, same layout on every page.** Every page is a title, a grey subtitle and white bordered cards with 11 to 12.5 px labels (`text-[11px]`, `text-[12px]` are used throughout), little contrast between primary and secondary content, no hero element. The result reads as a generic admin template rather than a chess product.

Secondary: the largest feature gaps against both sites are **clocks and time controls in Play, sounds, a free analysis board / FEN-PGN input, puzzle-style tactic training with themes, and a richer Insights page** (details below).

## 2. Review and analysis page

Current page: `frontend/src/features/review/*`. Layout is board with eval bar on the left; on the right a header card (title, opening, your accuracy, eval graph, "Retry your mistakes"), a tabbed panel (Summary, Moves, Key moments, Engine, Explorer) and a "move card" panel at the bottom.

### R1. Explain each key move in words, not only a percentage (H, M)
* Other sites: Chess.com's Coach explains why a move is good or bad and stops at key moments (Chess.com features overview; help article "How does Game Review work?"; redesign post says Coach was moved to the top to make explanations easier to read and to make Retry, best move and best continuation easier). A third-party comparison (mychessplan.com, a vendor, so treat with care) lists as the main complaint about Lichess that it "tells you a move is a mistake but doesn't explain why in words". The same article notes that Chess.com explanations are paywalled, so a free local app that explains is a real differentiator.
* MeChess now: `MoveCard.tsx` says "Gave away about 18% of the win chance. The engine preferred Nf3." plus the stored lines.
* Why it matters: this is the single biggest gap between "an engine printout" and "a coach".
* Change: MeChess already computes "what it allows" for practice (`features/coach/Feedback.tsx`: checkmate, a lost piece, the engine's line). Reuse that same feedback object in the review move card for each bad move and for missed chances ("You missed Qxf7#", "This leaves the bishop on c4 hanging", "Mate in 3 was available"). Add one sentence of consequence under the class label, and a "Show why" expand for the engine line. Keep the percentage as a secondary figure.

### R2. Key-moment walk-through with a persistent coach card (H, M)
* Other sites: Chess.com Game Review has "Next" which jumps to the next key move; the first key move is usually the last book move, with the opening name; Retry and "Show best" sit in the pinned coach card (help article "How does Game Review work?").
* MeChess now: a "Key moments" tab lists only your mistakes/blunders/misses (`Review.tsx`, `mistakes`); the move card is the third panel at the bottom of the sidebar; nothing guides you game-start to game-end.
* Why: players review by following a story, not by reading a table.
* Change: (a) pin the move card at the top of the sidebar (below the board header) so it is always visible; (b) add a primary "Next key moment" button under the board (and keys `N`/`P`) that visits, in order: last book move, each brilliant/great move of yours, each of your mistakes/misses, and the opponent's blunders you punished or missed; (c) in the Key moments tab, include great and brilliant moves and the opponent's errors, not only yours, with a filter (All / Mine / Opponent's).

### R3. Fix the sidebar so the Moves list is always on screen (H, S)
* Other sites: Lichess keeps the move list permanently visible next to the board with the graph above it; Chess.com keeps the move list and the coach card both visible.
* MeChess now (review screenshot, current look): the default tab is Summary, whose "Move kind" table is cut off after "Mistake" (Miss and Blunder are below the fold) because the middle panel is squeezed between the header card and the move card. The move list is hidden behind a tab you must click, and the page has three stacked cards fighting for 900 px.
* Change: default to the Moves tab; make Summary a one-time "report card" view (a button "Report card" in the header, or shown before the first step like Chess.com's highlights screen, which opens with the game graph, a one-line coach summary and accuracy per player). Give the move list a fixed minimum height and let the report card scroll as a whole, or move Summary into a modal.

### R4. Make the evaluation graph a real chart (H, S)
* Other sites: Lichess has an advantage chart plus a separate move-times chart (forum thread "Enhancing the lichess post-game analysis experience"); Chess.com's graph sits at the top with performance rating and accuracy (design update post).
* MeChess now: `EvalGraph.tsx` is a 72 px tall SVG, no hover, no labels, only your own mistakes marked.
* Change: 110 to 130 px tall; hover shows move number, SAN, evaluation and class; mark both players' blunders and brilliant/great moves with the same class colours as the move list; faint vertical lines for the phase boundaries (opening/middlegame/endgame already exist in the analysis); the playhead follows the board. Cheap and very visible.

### R5. Clock and time-per-move chart (M, M/L)
* Other sites: Lichess shows a move-times graph (forum thread above); Chess.com Game Review has a "time per move" display option under Interface > Review (help article).
* MeChess now: nothing; `docs/game-analysis.md` lists clock use as a known limit. `Prepare` does talk about the opponent being short of time, so the concept exists.
* Change: keep `[%clk]` comments from imported PGNs (Lichess and Chess.com PGNs carry them; confirm the importer preserves them, not verified), store per-ply seconds, and draw a small bar strip under the eval graph; add a "time trouble" marker on moves played under 10% of the clock. If the clock is added to Play (see P1), it also feeds this.

### R6. Show the opening name live as you step, and where you left book (M, S)
* Other sites: Chess.com's first key move is your last book move and the coach bubble names the opening and how you do in it (help article); Lichess shows the opening name under the board in analysis (not verified on a current page, widely seen).
* MeChess now: the opening appears once in the header text (`Review.tsx` header line) and in the explorer page title; `Book` class moves have no icon in the move list (`QUIET` in `MoveList.tsx`).
* Change: a one-line chip under the board: "C50 Italian Game: Giuoco Piano" updating per ply, with "Left book at move 7" once the book ends. Data is already used for the `book` class.

### R7. Captured pieces and material difference beside the player names (M, S)
* Other sites: Lichess shows material difference next to each player (an ICCA guide notes Lichess shows the difference, not all captured pieces); Chess.com shows captured pieces (not verified in a current help page).
* MeChess now: `PlayerStrip` shows colour dot, name, rating and accuracy only; the Play page shows "You" and the bot name only.
* Change: derive captured pieces from the FEN and show small piece icons plus "+3" on both strips, in Review, Play and Coach boards.

### R8. Sounds (M, S)
* Other sites: both sites have move/capture/check/end sounds; Lichess FAQ has a section on sound being blocked by browsers and the mute icon; Chess.com customises sounds in themes (features overview).
* MeChess now: no sound code anywhere in `frontend/src`.
* Change: a tiny synthesised set (WebAudio, so no sound-file licences) for move, capture, check, castle, game end; a Settings toggle with default on in Play and Coach, default off in Review stepping (or only on explicit click), and handle the browser's autoplay restriction the way Lichess documents it (first click enables).

### R9. Keyboard help and more shortcuts (M, S)
* Other sites: Lichess lists shortcuts with `?` on study, analysis, puzzle and game pages (Lichess FAQ); `z` toggles Zen mode in a game (FAQ). Community extensions add keys for jumping between blunders/mistakes/inaccuracies (B, M, I), which shows demand.
* MeChess now: arrows, Home/End, F, B, Esc work (`Review.tsx`) but are only visible in tooltips; the command palette has no "flip", "next mistake" or "analyse" actions.
* Change: a `?` dialog; add `N`/`P` (next/previous key moment), `E` (engine on/off), `Space` (autoplay), `M` (next mistake); add the same actions to the command palette with their key shown.

### R10. Autoplay (L, S)
* Both Chess.com (Interface > Review: autoplay and delays, help article) and Lichess (autoplay link in the menu bar, features/analysis page) offer it. MeChess lacks it. Add Space to play/pause with a speed setting.

### R11. Analysis board from any FEN or PGN, plus board editor (M, M)
* Other sites: Lichess Tools menu: Analysis board, Board editor, Import game, Advanced search (lichess.org/analysis page navigation); the analysis board opens from a game, pasted PGN, FEN or empty board and supports variations, comments, glyphs, arrows, tablebase and a board editor with side to move, castling and en passant (third-party mirror of the Lila docs: mintlify.wiki/lichess-org/lila/features/analysis). Chess.com Practice has a "Custom Position" mode: paste a FEN or take one from a game (help article "What is Practice").
* MeChess now: Review needs a stored game; the Explorer page starts from the initial position and cannot be given a position; there is no editor and no "play from here".
* Change: one "Analysis" page that reuses the Review board with no game: paste FEN/PGN, set up a position (a simple editor: piece palette, side to move, castling), engine and explorer tabs, "Copy FEN" and "Copy PGN". Add "Play from here against the engine" (needs a start-position parameter in `/api/play/start`) and "Add to practice".

### R12. Variations shown in the move list, promote, delete (M, M)
* Other sites: Lichess variations can be played, deleted, or promoted to main line (mirror docs above).
* MeChess now: a side line is a single transient `variation` array in `Review.tsx`; it disappears on navigating, and is not shown in the move list.
* Change: keep a small tree of tried lines per game for the session, show them inline as indented "(...)" under the move, and let Escape/"Back to the game" keep working. Persisting notes is L (see R14).

### R13. Engine tab upgrades (M, S)
* Other sites: multi-line display (1 to 5) with playable lines; tablebase automatically at 7 or fewer pieces (Lichess features page lists a 7-piece tablebase; mirror docs).
* MeChess now: `EngineTab.tsx` plays only the first move when you click a line; no hover preview; arrows only for the top line; endgame tablebases are used only in Coach and need a 1 GB download.
* Change: make each move in a line clickable (play up to that move), draw secondary lines as thinner arrows, show depth and nodes in a muted footer, and consider the Lichess tablebase query only if the owner accepts a network call (it breaks the "no requests to other sites" rule, so list as optional).

### R14. Annotations and saved notes (L, M)
* Lichess supports comments, glyphs, arrows and square highlights saved into studies; Chess.com's analysis board allows comments, annotations, highlights and arrows (features overview). MeChess lets you draw arrows with right-click but nothing is saved.
* Change: a notes box per game and per position (stored locally), arrows saved with the position. Defer behind R1 to R4.

### R15. Game rating estimate and retry impact (M, S)
* Chess.com shows a performance/"Game Rating" estimate on the report (help article: "an estimate of your level of play based on that single game ... averages out close to your actual rating over time") and, after retrying key moves, shows how accuracy is affected (support text).
* MeChess shows accuracy only. Change: an estimated game performance with a visible "estimate" label, calibrated on the user's own library (rating vs accuracy regression over analysed games), and a live "accuracy if you had found these" after Retry. Needs data: do not show with fewer than about 30 analysed games.

### R16. Retry mistakes: smarter selection (L, S)
* Lichess's "Learn from your mistakes" picks moves by change in winning chances, does not flag opening moves that masters have played and accepts master candidate moves (Lichess blog "Learn from your mistakes"), and works on any game including the opponent's.
* MeChess: `useRetry` lists miss/mistake/blunder with loss at least 0.1 for your side only. Change: skip a mistake if the explorer shows the move is common in master/high-rated play, and offer a toggle "Include opponent's moves".

## 3. Learning: Coach, practice, tactics

### L1. First-run Coach is a wall of empty (H, S)
* Other sites: Lichess Practice shows sections of lesson cards with a progress line (lichess.org/practice); Chess.com Learn and Practice show named courses, drills and master games with a start button (help article "What is Practice").
* MeChess now (coach screenshot): five tabs, a large empty-state panel and a six-row "positions by box" chart full of zeros; Openings and Endgames tabs are empty with CLI/download instructions until data exists.
* Change: a Coach home that always has something to start: cards "Due today: N positions", "From your latest game: M mistakes to retry" (links to Review retry), "Endgame of the day", "Opening to fix (your weakest)". Hide the box chart under "Details" until there are positions. Show greyed example content with a "Try a sample position" button rather than a blank panel (the Evergreen sample game already exists for Review; reuse it).

### L2. A theme-based tactics trainer (H, L, start S)
* Other sites: Lichess puzzles with 100+ themes, a dashboard showing weakest themes, history with re-attempt of failed puzzles, Streak, Storm (3 minutes) and Racer (Lichess features page; mirror docs). Chess.com: Daily puzzle, Rated puzzles, Puzzle Rush, Custom Puzzles by theme, with free limits (Chess.com features overview).
* MeChess now: drills are only positions from your own mistakes, with no tactic theme.
* Why: puzzle practice is the daily habit on both sites; a study app without it feels incomplete.
* Change in two steps. Step 1 (S): tag each of your drill positions and mistakes with a simple theme (hanging piece, missed mate, missed fork/pin if the engine line captures material after a fork, back rank, missed capture) from the stored lines, show the tags as chips on the practice card, add a theme filter and a "Your tactics: found vs missed" block in Insights (Chess.com Insights has Tactics with found versus missed forks, pins and mates and a list of the actual positions). Step 2 (L): optional import of the public Lichess puzzle database for theme puzzles; check the licence of that data set before bundling (not verified here) and keep it a user-triggered download so the no-outside-requests rule stays intact.

### L3. Practice results screen and light progression (M, S)
* Chess.com awards 1 to 3 crowns on bot games depending on hints and undos used (help article on bots); Lichess Practice shows a percentage of completed lessons.
* MeChess: `Practice.tsx` ends with "Done: you found X of Y without a hint on the first try" in a one-line banner.
* Change: a real session summary: score ring, time, which positions failed with a mini board and "Retry now", and a streak counter derived from days with a completed session. Avoid heavy gamification (owner preference unknown); a streak and a count are enough.

### L4. Openings practice should not start with "paste a PGN" (M, M)
* Other sites: Chess.com Practice > Openings: pick an opening, a colour and engine strength and practise against a bot that plays the lines (help article "What is Practice"); Lichess opening list with names, popularity and results (lichess.org/opening).
* MeChess now: `Openings.tsx` asks you to save a repertoire as a PGN with variations in a textarea.
* Change: "Pick an opening" from the named-openings list (the opening names are already in the data), choose depth, auto-build a repertoire from the explorer's most-played moves at your rating band, with a preview, then practise. Keep PGN paste as an advanced option. Also add "Save as repertoire" from the Explorer page and from Prepare's plan (the plan already exists).

### L5. Endgame practice should work before the 1 GB download (M, M)
* Chess.com's Practice endgames are played against Stockfish with no downloads (help article).
* MeChess: `Endgames.tsx` shows "No endgame tablebases found" and stops. Change: allow the lesson against Stockfish with a clear "judged by the engine; install tablebases for perfect judgement" note; make the download an in-app button with progress (Activity page), not instructions.

### L6. Use the Books comments where they belong (M, M)
* MeChess indexes annotated book comments by position and idea (`BooksPage.tsx`); neither site has this. It sits in a separate "Books" page that a beginner will not understand. Change: in the Review move card show "What books say here" when the current position has a matching note, and in Coach weaknesses link a concept (for example "outpost") to the notes. Rename the page "Ideas" or fold it into Coach.

## 4. Openings and explorer

### O1. Explorer sources and filters (H, S/M)
* Other sites: Lichess openings page has a List tab and an Opening explorer tab, with speed and rating filters (lichess.org/opening); the global explorer is Masters + Lichess, and a personal explorer exists, also for other players' games (Lichess features page). Chess.com's explorer shows master moves with popularity trends, and a Game Explorer searches your own and others' games (features overview).
* MeChess now: one source (players by rating band) with a rating dropdown only (`ExplorerPage.tsx`); no total row, no average rating, no example games, no "your games here". 
* Change: (S) a "Your games" tab or extra column: your own result and count from the library at this position (Prepare already computes your record against an opponent's opening, so the query exists in spirit); (S) total W/D/B row and a game count on top; (S) speed filter next to the rating; (M) top example games from the explorer database with a link to import them; (S) hover a move row to draw its arrow on the board.

### O2. Opening names everywhere and a browsable opening list (M, M)
* Lichess's opening page lists openings with popularity, first move and results (page content above). MeChess has the names (Review header, Library column) but no page to browse openings by name or see your own results per opening (only a "your best" line on Home).
* Change: an "Openings" tab in Explorer: name, ECO, popularity, your games, your score and accuracy, sorted; click to load the position on the board.

### O3. Line breadcrumb should be clickable (L, S)
* On the Explorer page the line under the board is plain text (`sanLine`). Make each move a link back to that position, as every move list is. Add keyboard arrows like Review.

## 5. Insights and profile

### I1. Insights is thin compared with both sites (H, M)
* Lichess Insights (blog): you choose a metric (average centipawn loss, move time, result, rating gain, opponent rating, moves per game, piece moved, opportunism, luck) against a dimension (phase, result, colour, opening ECO, opponent strength, piece moved, move time, castling side, queen trade, material imbalance), with filters; each answer is a chart and a table, and the URL is shareable. Chess.com Insights (help article): Games (over time, accuracy by move number, results by opponent rating, similar players, past performance), Openings (your 10 most played as White and Black), Tactics (found vs missed with position lists), Moves (move quality over time, accuracy by piece, castling), Calendar (by time of day and day of week), Geography.
* MeChess now (`Insights.tsx`): activity heatmap, rating line, accuracy by phase, move quality bar, results by colour/speed. Opportunism and luck are computed (`docs/game-analysis.md`) but only appear in the Prepare report, not for you.
* Change, in order of value per hour: (1) an **Openings table** (opening, colour, games, score, accuracy, average loss in the first 12 moves) with click-through to the Library filtered by that opening; (2) **a filter bar** for the whole page (time class, colour, last 30/90/365 days) held in the URL so it can be bookmarked; (3) **Move quality over time** (you have per-game class counts; the Home cards already compute "vs the 20 before"); (4) **Time of day / weekday** results; (5) **Opportunism / luck / conversion / resourcefulness** for yourself with one-line explanations; (6) tactics found versus missed once L2 step 1 exists. Every bar should be clickable to show those games.

### I2. Rating chart (H, S)
* The chart (`charts.tsx` `LineChart`, phone screenshot) has no axis, tooltip, time-class selector or markers and shows only first and last dates plus "low/high"; a one-game library renders a flat line, and a short library says "Not enough games yet" in a large empty card.
* Change: y-axis ticks, hover tooltip (date, rating, opponent, result), one line per time class with tabs (Blitz/Rapid/Bullet), 30/90/all range, and dots for the games. Empty state with fewer than 5 games should be one line, not a card.

### I3. Give the profile a real home (M, S)
* Both sites have a profile showing rating per time class, games, result split and recent form. In MeChess the "Your profile" link in the sidebar goes to Settings (`Shell.tsx`), and Home shows only the main speed in a small card.
* Change: a header block at the top of Insights: avatar initials, name(s) and sites, ratings per time class with games count and 30-day delta, W/D/L stacked bar. Link the sidebar profile to it.

### I4. Home cards read as unfinished (M, S)
* Home screenshot: "needs more games" under two cards, a vertical bar for "Results", the text "1 wins · 393 draws · 6 losses" (a plural bug in `Home.tsx`).
* Change: pluralise, replace the Results bar with a horizontal W/D/L stacked bar, hide "needs more games" lines in favour of "Analyse N more games to see this" with a button, and show a dash instead of a number when there is nothing to show.

## 6. Play

### P1. No clocks or time controls (H, L)
* Both sites play with clocks and increments; Chess.com's bot setup lets you pick a time control or none (help article "How can I play against the Chess.com bots?"); Lichess supports UltraBullet to Classical (features page).
* MeChess now (`Play.tsx`, play screenshot): only opponent, level and colour. No clock, no flag, no time pressure, so you cannot practise the thing the Prepare report says opponents lose on.
* Change: untimed plus presets (3+2, 5+3, 10+0, 15+10) and custom minutes/increment; the engine's thinking time scales with its remaining time; flag on timeout; store `[%clk]` in the saved PGN so Review (R5) can chart it. Premoves become worthwhile only after this (both sites have them).

### P2. In-game help toggles (H, M)
* Chess.com bots: evaluation bar, threat arrows (red), suggestion arrows (green), move feedback against the best move, and an engine display, each a toggle; hints and undos lower the reward (help article above).
* MeChess: only Take back and Resign. Change: an "Assist" menu with Hint (draw the best move, record "assisted" on the game), Eval bar, Show last-move quality, and keep Take back; show an "assisted" tag in the Library so honest stats stay honest.

### P3. Opponent choice (M, S)
* Chess.com: 100+ named bots in tiers plus an Engine slider 250 to 3200 (help article); adaptive bots that ease off after your mistakes. Lichess: levels 1 to 8.
* MeChess: eight Stockfish levels and a slider for the human-like bot (`Play.tsx`). The human-like bot is already better than anything on either site for realism. Change: label each Stockfish level with an approximate rating range (measure it once with the app's own analysis, and label it "approx."), give each level or rating a short friendly profile (style: "tactical", "cautious") taken from the model's style data, and show the level in the header strip. Adaptive mode is optional (L).

### P4. Game-over flow (M, S)
* Chess.com shows a game-over panel with a big Game Review button; Lichess offers rematch, analysis and new opponent.
* MeChess has "Review the game" and "New game". Change: add "Rematch (swap colours)", start the analysis in the background on game end and show "Analysing 40%" on the button; auto-open the review when ready.

### P5. Player strips and move list in Play (M, S)
* The strips say only "You" and the engine name; the move list has no last-move highlight or click-to-navigate and no copy-PGN. Add rating/level, captured material (R7), clock (P1); make the list match the Review list so players are not surprised.

### P6. Start from a position or an opening (M, M)
* Chess.com Practice vs Computer allows a custom position or opening (help article on bot custom positions, referenced from the bots page). MeChess cannot. Covered by R11.

## 7. Library

### Lib1. Sorting, more filters, bulk actions, mini boards (M, S/M)
* Lichess has advanced game search and import/export of PGN (features page). Chess.com offers archive search and filters (not verified in a current help page).
* MeChess (`Library.tsx`, library screenshot): search, colour, result and analysed filters only; columns cannot be sorted; no time-control or opening facet, no date range; no select/export/delete; rows show text only. The sample's date column shows a bare "2026", mixed with "28 Dec 2025".
* Change: clickable column headers to sort (date, accuracy, opponent rating); speed and date-range filters; "Games with mistakes" and "Has a retry" filters; a small board thumbnail of the final or critical position as the leading element of each row (an `aspect-square` 40 px board is cheap and changes the feel of the page); row checkboxes for analyse/export PGN/delete; consistent dates.

## 8. Navigation, layout, polish

### N1. Flat default board (H, S)
* Change the default from "Icy (grain)" to a flat, medium-contrast theme (a green or blue-grey with no grain) and make the texture an opt-in "Texture" toggle. Check contrast of the coordinates and the last-move/check highlights on it. This is the highest value per minute of any item in this report.

### N2. Home: actions first, checklist later (H, S)
* Chess.com's home leads with Play and Review actions; Lichess opens on play. MeChess Home (screenshot) leads with four statistics, then a mini-board "Next step" card, recent games, and a "Your workspace" checklist about Stockfish, tablebases and the explorer database.
* Change: three large action tiles at the top: "Review your last game" (with its accuracy), "Practice (N due)", "Play a game". Move the workspace checklist to Settings > "Setup" and show it on Home only when something required is missing; hide optional items. Put recent games before the statistics.

### N3. Remove developer-speak from the UI (H, S)
* The places: `ExplorerPage.tsx` ("see docs/opening-explorer.md"), `BooksPage.tsx` ("Run `chessme books-learn` (docs/language-model.md)"), `Endgames.tsx` ("folder named syzygy"), `BotPage.tsx` (environment variable and Keychain instructions), "Opening explorer: not built (optional...)" on Home.
* Change: replace each with a button that does the thing ("Download opening data", "Add endgame tables") using the existing jobs system and Activity page; keep the technical text under a "Details" disclosure. Rename "Bot" to "Lichess bot" in the sidebar and put it under Settings or "More" (it is a niche feature that occupies a top-level slot).

### N4. First-run onboarding (M, S/M)
* Chess.com asks about level and goals at sign-up; Lichess has none. MeChess has no guided start; the checklist on Home is the closest.
* Change: a three-step first-run sheet: (1) your user names and rating (already in Settings), (2) import with a progress bar, (3) "Analysing your last game ... open it". Plus a "Try the sample game" link on empty Library and Insights using the bundled sample.

### N5. Loading and empty states (M, S)
* `App.tsx` uses a bare "Loading…" Suspense fallback; Review uses proper skeletons. Use the same skeleton blocks for every lazy route. Empty states should show what the feature will look like (a faded example), not a large icon over text.

### N6. Mobile (M, M)
* Phone screenshots: the bottom bar holds six items (Home, Library, Coach, Play, Explorer, Settings) so Insights, Prepare, Books, Activity and Bot are unreachable except through the palette; in Review the tab bar is cut ("Exp..."), the eval bar and board are small, and the move list is far below the fold.
* Change: a "More" sheet in the bottom bar; scrollable tab list (or icons); a horizontal one-line move strip directly under the board with large Back/Forward buttons; sticky "Retry/Next key moment" bar. Re-take phone screenshots in the current look.

### N7. Typography and density (M, S)
* Many labels are 11 to 12.5 px with the `text-faint` colour. Raise secondary text to 13 px, labels to 12 px minimum, and check `--faint` contrast in all eight palette/mode combinations (WCAG AA 4.5:1). Use one stronger type step for page titles (the page title and subtitle are close in weight) and give the board pages less chrome.

### N8. Settings structure (L, S)
* Chess.com puts review options under Interface > Review: arrows, key-move highlighting, on-board classification icons, autoplay and delay, coach avatar, time per move display (help article). Lichess has Game display and Game behaviour preferences (FAQ mentions Zen mode and auto-threefold claim).
* MeChess Settings is one long column (accounts, engine, board and pieces, opening cache) with the board preview floating at the side. Add an "Interface" card: show class icons on the board, default best-move arrow, autoplay speed, piece animation speed, highlight legal moves, show evaluation in the move list by default, confirm before resign, sounds. Add a sticky in-page index on the left.

### N9. Visual identity (M, M)
* The page chrome is generic: pale grey canvas, white cards with 1 px borders, one green accent, a lucide icon on everything. The board and pieces carry no brand. Make the board larger and the sidebar quieter, use the class colours more deliberately (they are the one place colour means something), and add a few chess-specific touches: opening diagrams in lists, a king/knight mark for the empty states instead of generic outline icons, tabular numerals for all numbers, a heavier weight for key numbers (accuracy).

## 11. What MeChess does better already (keep these)

* **Prepare against an opponent** (`features/prepare`): traps, a plan tree with expected scores and "gamble" markers, saved as a repertoire. Lichess has a personal explorer that can be pointed at other players' games (features page); I found no equivalent plan builder on either site. This is a genuine differentiator; give it more space in the navigation, not less.
* **Graded practice feedback with reasons**, spaced repetition from your own mistakes, "try for the best" after an acceptable move (`Practice.tsx`). Chess.com's custom puzzles from your blunders sit behind paid tiers (comparison article); Lichess's "Learn from your mistakes" is one pass without scheduling.
* **Retry your mistakes inside the review** rather than in a separate place, with a one-click "try this line" from every stored engine line (`MoveCard.tsx`).
* **Honest, documented analysis**: Lichess's accuracy formula and win-probability curve, volatility-weighted and harmonic means, fixed-node budget so results are reproducible, thresholds that follow the published Chess.com bands, and a docs page that says what is judgement (`docs/game-analysis.md`). Chess.com users complain about unexplained "brilliant" and "great" labels (forum threads on the brilliant-move rules); MeChess states its rules.
* **Unlimited, free, private, local analysis** with Stockfish 19, no daily review cap and no ads or upsell prompts (the main complaints about Chess.com's free tier in the comparison articles).
* **The human-like bot** ("MeChess plays like a person") and its rating-aware default level (`Play.tsx`).
* **Explorer defaults to your own rating band** and shows reference counts of mistakes by rating band, speed, phase and situation (Coach Weaknesses); both are personalised in a way the sites' generic explorers are not.
* **Command palette (Ctrl/Cmd+K)** that jumps to pages and finds your own games by player or opening: neither site has one.
* **Board component**: pointer-event drag with no React renders while dragging, move animation, keyboard-operable board, promotion picker, right-click arrows and marks (`src/board`), four site palettes in light and dark, nine board colours, six bundled piece sets and your own images. The customisation is broader than either site's and meets the accessibility bar better than most.
* **Library at 10,000+ games** with a virtualised list, instant filters and an Activity page with progress and cancel for background jobs.
* **Opening cache** so stepping through a game shows stored lines instantly while the engine goes deeper.
* **Home trend deltas** ("vs the 20 before") and the activity calendar.
